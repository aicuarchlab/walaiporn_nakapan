Dr. Walaiporn Nakapan is an architect and Assistant Professor at the Faculty of Architecture, Chulalongkorn University. Her design practice centres on computational and parametric design and digital fabrication — taking form-finding through machine-made components to full-scale, buildable, reversible structures.

A single-material pavilion in birch marine plywood, developed from Origami principles: flat sheets cut and folded until the folds themselves carry the load — no heavy post-and-beam frame behind them. Folding turns a thin sheet into a structural shell, cutting material volume and making the whole thing flat-packable.

Fabrication, assembly, disassembly, transport and reassembly were part of the brief from the first sketch, not an afterthought. Students from several countries carried the work from computational form-finding, through machine-cut components, to hand assembly on site in eleven days.

01b · 2026 · Shougang Park, Beijing · Design for reversibility · On permanent display

From Shenzhen to Beijing — the pavilion’s second site

จากเสิ่นเจิ้นสู่ปักกิ่ง

When the school ended, the pavilion was dismantled, moved across the country and reassembled at Shougang Park in Beijing, where it remains with no scheduled date for dismantling.

That journey is the argument: a folded plate system detailed for fabrication, assembly, disassembly, transport and reassembly does not end when its programme does. Design for reversibility extends the life of a temporary installation and lets it acquire a new presence in another place — a public park rather than a university lobby.

A full-scale prototype, 6.00 × 6.00 m and 2.00 m high, assembled from 114 EPS foam blocks. The geometry came from compression-only equilibrium form-finding with Thrust Network Analysis, run through the RhinoVAULT plug-in: the shell resolves its loads in compression, so no tensile reinforcement is needed.

Phase 1 established that the method holds for lightweight materials — and exposed two limits that set the brief for Phase 2.

Compression-Only Pavilion from 3D-Printed Recycled PET Bottle Filament, with Holographic Assembly

พาวิลเลียนรับแรงอัดอย่างเดียวจากเส้นพลาสติกรีไซเคิลจากขวดเพ็ท และการประกอบแบบโฮโลกราฟิก

Most reuse of PET waste in architecture is straightforward substitution. This project inverts the question: can the properties of the recycled material govern the design logic instead? Post-consumer bottles are cleaned, ground and extruded into printable filament.

Form-finding runs through Thrust Network Analysis in RhinoVAULT; the shell is subdivided into modules bounded by print-bed volume and fabricated by fused filament fabrication. Assembly is holographic — a smartphone running Fologram overlays the digital model onto the physical work in real time, guiding position and sequence and making tolerance error visible. Reversible interlocking joints replace permanent adhesive, so components can be re-ground and re-extruded inside a closed material loop.

Through-lines

What the projects share

FOLD

Geometry as structure

Origami folding turns thin plywood sheets into a self-supporting shell — stiffness from form rather than added frames.

FORM-FIND

Compression-only shells

Thrust Network Analysis in RhinoVAULT finds funicular forms that need no tensile reinforcement, in foam and in printed plastic.

REVERSE

Design for disassembly

Joints and sequences detailed so a structure can be taken apart, moved and rebuilt — Shenzhen to Beijing is the proof.

CIRCULAR

Material governs logic

Recycled PET bottle filament, measured for embodied carbon, in a closed loop where modules can be re-ground and re-printed.

ASSEMBLE

Holographic guidance

Fologram overlays the digital model on the physical build, so hand assembly becomes precise and repeatable.
